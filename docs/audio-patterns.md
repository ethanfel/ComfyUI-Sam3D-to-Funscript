# Audio beat patterns

Motion Studio has an **Audio · beat patterns** row below Main and Source tracks. It works in connected editors and newly downloaded offline viewers. A drum stem generally gives clearer timing than a full mix; source separation is not required by this feature.

For audio-only authoring, load the video, leave the Processing Timeline node on **prepare**, and run once. Motion Studio opens with a neutral Main curve, video and device previews, and the audio row. You do not need to run SAM3D, create tracking regions, or use Automatic. Save/export works immediately. You can add tracking later; your audio blocks and authored Main curve are preserved.

1. Click **Choose beat audio** and select your FLAC, WAV, MP3 or other browser-supported audio file. Analysis runs locally and shows its waveform, beat markers, drum hits and an estimated tempo.
2. Click **Use current video's audio** to analyze the loaded video's soundtrack as the full mix. Stabilized previews use their original video, since stabilization renders omit audio. Alternatively, **Choose full mix** accepts an audio file or a browser-decodable MP4. Leave **Use full mix for shape and energy** enabled for dual analysis.
3. Check alignment against the video. **Audio starts at** and **Full mix starts at** shift each audio clock independently in milliseconds: positive values delay it; negative values advance it. The video button aligns the soundtrack to the project's source time, including stabilization trims and delayed audio timestamps in server-loaded videos. A manually chosen full mix initially uses the drum offset. A full-source stem defaults to the project's source-time offset when the video was trimmed.
4. Click **Select whole video** to generate for the full clip, or set **In / Out** directly below the audio waveform. Shift-dragging also selects a range; it stays synchronized with Main. Click **New block** when creating another section.
5. Choose **Suggest from audio**, **Choose pattern**, or **Random variation**, then click **Generate preview**. The pink dashed curve is temporary. Random mode has a repeatable seed and **Another variation** button. **Why these shapes?** explains each phrase's choice.
6. Click **Save new block** or **Replace selected block**. Saved blocks occupy only their selected time ranges. They do not overlap; selecting an existing block exposes its settings again.
7. Click **Use block in Main L0** (or the currently selected axis) to include it in the device preview and funscript export. Main's existing join/blend setting applies. Main must be unlocked. Other axes and source curves are preserved.

**Drum peaks** is the default: strokes follow each detected drum hit at its measured loudness peak, including syncopation. **Detected beats** follows an estimated regular pulse, snapping matched hits to their peaks; inferred pulses may fall between actual hits. **Tempo grid** lets you enter 30–300 BPM; its phase follows the first detected attack. **Beats / cycle** controls how many timing intervals form one complete pattern. One is the default: a full stroke per selected beat. Two gives one full stroke per two beats, so the primary downstroke lands on every other beat.

**On beat → Down · lowest point** is the default. The motion curve reaches its low point at each cycle’s starting drum peak. **Up · highest point** and **Original shape** remain available; saved blocks retain their original direction and timing until regenerated. Secondary pattern accents use subdivisions within the cycle. Peak timing needs current audio analysis: older projects keep their saved analysis, and choosing the beat file again refines the timing without replacing saved blocks or Main.

With a drum stem alone, suggestions use local beat rate, attack density and strength. With a full mix, compact features describe sustained energy, energy changes, bass power, brightness and attack activity. Combined with drum strength, density and offbeat hits, these rank ten shapes every eight beats: Smooth Bounce, Sine Wave, Half Stroke, Staircase Up/Down, Double Tap, Quick Rise, Hold High, Accent & Echo and Swing. For example, a build favors Staircase Up; sustained passages favor Sine Wave; dense attacks favor Double Tap. Random mode chooses among up to three highly ranked shapes for the same phrase. Drum-only random mode retains its tempo-based pool.

Timing always comes from the drum input, including when using Drum peaks or Tempo grid. Full-mix events never move beat markers. Blue shows the drum waveform, purple the full-mix envelope and gold the drum timing. Phrases with less than 80% full-mix coverage use drum-only analysis and say so in **Why these shapes?**. Use the independent offsets to correct alignment for separately trimmed stems or files with different leading silence.

These are signal-based heuristics, not learned recognition of melody, genre, choreography or verse/chorus boundaries. An eight-beat phrase is a grouping on the chosen timing grid. Review the preview and try half/double timing or a manual tempo when the estimated pulse is wrong. The 14 manual beat shapes include Triangle, Sine Wave and the twelve additional rhythm shapes; the ordinary pattern generator offers all 26 shapes.

**Follow audio energy** varies amplitude using cycle-level energy, with smooth transitions. Dual mode combines 70% full-mix energy with 30% drum energy where the mix covers the cycle. Quiet drum cycles or a silent full mix fade toward the chosen center, including with Follow audio energy disabled. Amplitude and center remain editable; values are bounded to 0–100. Exact collinear point reduction keeps generated files smaller without altering their piecewise-linear curves.

Audio blocks, both analyses, both offsets, choice explanations and generation settings are saved with the project and shared between linked editors. The raw audio is not uploaded or embedded. Playback uses the video audio. Reopening the project does not need either audio file to regenerate blocks from saved analysis. Replacing or removing an input or changing its offset leaves saved blocks intact until you preview and replace a block. Uncheck **Use full mix for shape and energy** to compare drum-only suggestions without removing the mix.

For server-loaded videos, the video button extracts and caches a small mono WAV under the ComfyUI output directory's `sam3d_funscript/audio-previews` folder. Only audio is decoded and transferred for analysis; the browser does not download the entire video for this operation. File size and modification time invalidate the cache. The first audio stream is used, and timestamp gaps are preserved. In an offline viewer, first choose the source video in the preview; its soundtrack is decoded locally with no upload. For stabilized offline projects, choose the original video in the Original view. Unsupported or silent videos show an error and keep the previous analysis. Cancelling or changing projects also leaves saved audio inputs and blocks intact.

**Remove block** removes it from the audio row. Copies already in Main are independent: use **Applied patterns → Remove pattern** to restore the previous Main section, or use **Undo**. Undo also covers audio import, offset changes and block edits. A same-video processing rerun preserves the audio lane; changing to a different video starts a separate project.
