# Audio beat patterns

Motion Studio has an **Audio · beat patterns** row below Main and Source tracks. It works in connected editors and newly downloaded offline viewers. A drum stem generally gives clearer timing than a full mix; source separation is not required by this feature.

For audio-only authoring, load the video, leave the Processing Timeline node on **prepare**, and run once. Motion Studio opens with a neutral Main curve, video and device previews, and the audio row. You do not need to run SAM3D, create tracking regions, or use Automatic. Save/export works immediately. You can add tracking later; your audio blocks and authored Main curve are preserved.

1. Click **Choose beat audio** and select your FLAC, WAV, MP3 or other browser-supported audio file. Analysis runs locally and shows its waveform, beat markers, drum hits and an estimated tempo.
2. Click **Use current video's audio** to analyze the loaded video's soundtrack as the full mix. Stabilized previews use their original video, since stabilization renders omit audio. Alternatively, **Choose full mix** accepts an audio file or a browser-decodable MP4. Leave **Use full mix for shape and energy** enabled for dual analysis.
3. Check alignment against the video. **Audio starts at** and **Full mix starts at** shift each audio clock independently in milliseconds: positive values delay it; negative values advance it. The video button aligns the soundtrack to the project's source time, including stabilization trims and delayed audio timestamps in server-loaded videos. A manually chosen full mix initially uses the drum offset. A full-source stem defaults to the project's source-time offset when the video was trimmed.
4. Click **Select whole video** to generate for the full clip, or set **In / Out** directly below the audio waveform. Shift-dragging also selects a range; it stays synchronized with Main. Click **New block** when creating another section.
5. Start with **Rhythm → Simplify groove**. Set density, low-hit emphasis and the maximum hit rate, then choose **Auto · match sound**, **Suggest from audio**, **Choose pattern**, or **Random variation** and click **Generate preview**. **Listen rhythm** auditions a clean percussion version of the chosen timing. The pink dashed curve is temporary. Random mode has a repeatable seed and **Another variation** button. **Why these shapes?** explains each phrase's choice.
6. Click **Save new block** or **Replace audio in range**. A replacement overwrites all saved audio within In / Out, including a range spanning several blocks, and keeps the audio outside it. To redo the whole song, click **Select whole video → Generate preview → Replace audio in range**. You do not need to select or delete old blocks first. **Undo** restores the previous blocks. Selecting an existing block exposes its settings again.
7. Click **Use selection in Main L0** (or the currently selected axis) to include that exact In / Out range in the device preview and funscript export. Main's existing join/blend setting applies. Main must be unlocked. Other axes and source curves are preserved.

Saved audio shows the same section boundaries and labels as the Source row, plus scene-cut boundaries. This also works when the audio was generated for the whole video before tracking was added: the displayed splits reuse its saved curve. Both rows share the same ruler, zoom and scrolling. Click an audio section to select its exact range, then **Use selection in Main**. To use SAM3D instead, click its matching Source section and **Copy selection · all axes**. You can then copy the audio selection again without regenerating it. Only ranges fully covered by saved audio can be copied; missing audio is never bridged silently. The saved-block dropdown still selects an entire original block when needed.

To send several audio sections to Main together, click the first block label and **Shift-click** the last. **Ctrl-click** (or **Cmd-click** on macOS) adds or removes individual blocks, including blocks separated by other sections. The highlighted blocks and **Use N blocks in Main L0** button show what will be copied. Unselected intervals and gaps keep their existing Main motion; adjacent selected blocks are copied as one continuous range, with blending only at its outer edges. A single **Undo** restores the whole transfer. Click one block, edit In / Out, or use **Select whole video** to return to generating patterns for a time range.

The **Rhythm** menu adds three deterministic modes; no AI model is needed:

- **Follow accents** keeps locally prominent attacks and suppresses weak or crowded hits. Retained hits stay at their measured loudness peaks.
- **Simplify groove** (the default for new work) also favors the pulse and wider spacing. **Keep syncopation** lets strong offbeat hits remain at their original times; disabling it filters off-pulse hits rather than moving them.
- **Steady pulse** reconstructs a more regular pulse and follows local tempo changes gradually. It can insert pulses between measured hits. Use **Timing → Tempo grid** and set BPM when the estimate is wrong.
- **Original timing** keeps the prior hit/beat/tempo behavior and all Beats / cycle choices. Existing saved blocks load this mode unless they were generated with one of the new modes.

**Density** controls how many accents survive (or the pulse subdivision in Steady pulse). **Favor low hits** weights low-frequency attacks over high percussion; mid frequencies remain available. The analyzer measures separate low (<250 Hz), mid (250–2000 Hz), and high bands. These are approximate frequency groups, not learned kick/snare labels. The pulse estimate uses the same emphasis. The three new modes generate one full pattern per selected event. **Max hits / s** sets minimum event spacing; it does not limit secondary accents within a shape, acceleration, or physical device speed. Stroke length changes travel, and the existing **Device output** controls remain the place to constrain physical travel/speed. Long gaps without selected events fade to center.

**Percussion sound** selects the actual sound used by **Listen rhythm** and WAV export. Click **Preview sound** beside the selector to hear three sample hits immediately, even before loading beat audio or generating a motion block. Click **Stop preview** to stop early, or choose another sound and preview it. In Auto mode it samples the drum hit nearest the playhead using the current preview, saved selection, or loaded drum analysis; without any hit data it clearly labels a default kick. This short preview leaves curves and selections unchanged. **Auto · match drum stem** is the default. It approximates each retained drum hit using low/mid/high spectral power, tonal character, pitch and decay, and can layer a low drum with high percussion. It always uses the drum stem; the full mix remains available for motion shape and energy. The hint lists the chosen sounds for the current preview or saved selection.

Choose a fixed sound to audition the same rhythm with **Deep kick**, **Punchy kick**, **Low tom**, **High tom**, **Dry snare**, **Soft snare**, **Clap**, **Closed hat**, **Open hat**, **Shaker**, **Rim**, **Woodblock**, **Click**, or **Low percussion**. These are deterministic synthesized sounds, with no sample downloads or AI model. Auto adjusts pitch and decay within each sound's range. It is an approximate replacement, not a recreation of the recording or a reliable instrument classifier; manual choices are available when the match sounds wrong.

Sound selection is saved with the project's viewing preferences and does not invalidate a motion preview, edit saved blocks or change Main. **Listen rhythm** plays the selected percussion from the preview's saved event timestamps. Each synthesized hit's loudest 5 ms envelope aligns with its primary cycle event, including **Down · lowest point**. Legacy Click and Low percussion retain their sample-peak alignment. Overlapping tails can change the combined envelope, and audio at the selected edges is clipped to the range. Other accents within a complex shape are not additional timing clicks. With Original shape selected, those events represent cycle starts rather than a guaranteed low/high point. The separate player starts at the selected In time; its own clock starts at zero. Pausing, seeking and replaying are available in its controls. Changing the range/settings, starting video playback, or leaving the tab stops the audition.

**Download rhythm WAV** exports that same percussion. Place the WAV at the selection's **In** time to align it with the video. **Download timing JSON** contains event times relative to the WAV, their absolute `video_at` timestamps, and `start_ms` / `end_ms`. Version 2 also includes the selected sound and each hit's resolved percussion voices, pitch, decay and gain. Recipes are deterministic; JSON and WAV resolve the same saved events. `ranges_ms` records the selected intervals relative to the WAV. A multi-block selection retains its gaps as silence, including trimming long percussion tails at unselected blocks. Preview sounds are replacements for audition/export, not replacements for the source soundtrack. The event list drives both the curve and the sound directly; generated audio is never re-analyzed.

For full timbre matching on an existing project, choose the beat file again, then **Generate preview**. Analysis version 4 saves compact per-hit timbre descriptors, and newly generated blocks keep their own copy with source timing/offset information. Reconstructed pulses can borrow a nearby hit's timbre while retaining the reconstructed event time. Older blocks use their saved band balance when available, or a default kick; the hint identifies this fallback. Reimporting the file alone keeps existing blocks unchanged. Regenerate and save only the blocks you want to replace. The analysis and saved events work in the offline viewer without the audio file.

Previously saved analyses still work through a broadband fallback, with an on-screen reminder to choose the beat file again for multiband measurements. Existing blocks and Main stay unchanged until you explicitly generate and save a replacement. Once imported with the new analysis, the compact attacks and saved timing events travel with the project and work offline without the original audio file. Older blocks without timing events need a new preview before rhythm audition/export is available.

**Original timing → Drum peaks** preserves the previous behavior: strokes follow each detected drum hit at its measured loudness peak, including syncopation. **Detected beats** follows an estimated regular pulse, snapping matched hits to their peaks; inferred pulses may fall between actual hits. **Tempo grid** lets you enter 30–300 BPM; its phase follows the first detected attack. With **Original timing**, **Beats / cycle** controls how many timing intervals form one complete pattern. One is the default: a full stroke per selected beat. Two gives one full stroke per two beats, so the primary downstroke lands on every other beat.

**On beat → Down · lowest point** is the default. The motion curve reaches its low point at each cycle’s starting drum peak. **Up · highest point** and **Original shape** remain available; saved blocks retain their original direction and timing until regenerated. Secondary pattern accents use subdivisions within the cycle. Peak timing needs current audio analysis: older projects keep their saved analysis, and choosing the beat file again refines the timing without replacing saved blocks or Main.

Open **Pattern catalogue · 28 shapes** to browse curve thumbnails grouped into **Flowing**, **Pulses**, **Holds**, **Accents**, and **Steps**. Click a card to select it in **Choose pattern** mode, then Generate preview. The dropdown also groups all shapes by family. Fourteen new templates add Soft Pulse, Rounded Swing, Early Lift, Late Lift, Sharp Rebound, Delayed Return, Double Bounce, Triplet Bounce, Gallop, Reverse Echo, Decaying Ripples, Step & Hold, Four Step Rise and Four Step Fall.

**Auto · match sound** compares the sound's volume envelope with the catalogue templates. Use the Mode dropdown, or open the catalogue and click **Auto match this range**. With the full mix enabled, its envelope guides the choice; otherwise it uses the drum stem. This is a comparison of how loudness rises, holds, falls, and repeats within cycles, not a direct copy of the audio signal's rapid oscillations. The drum input still supplies every primary timing event. Down/Up on beat is retained.

The matcher samples complete cycles, normalizes their loudness range, and compares envelope shape and changes in slope. It picks one template every eight timing intervals, so a phrase keeps its shape instead of switching at every hit. **Pattern family** restricts which shapes it may choose. Changes happen on cycle boundaries. A nearly flat or silent envelope shows an explicit fallback rather than a match score. Full-mix coverage below 80% of the phrase falls back to drums and says so; both offsets are respected. Existing saved waveform analysis is sufficient for this mode, including in an offline viewer.

**Why these shapes?** lists the selected pattern, envelope source, template-similarity score and the next three candidates. Matching cards are highlighted and labelled **matched** in the catalogue. Similarity describes a numerical envelope fit, not musical understanding or a probability that the motion is correct. Review the curve and choose another card if needed. Secondary accents in a matched shape remain subdivisions within each primary cycle; the rhythm audition still clicks only at primary events. The existing stroke length, energy and device-output controls continue to apply.

**Random variation** uses the selected catalogue family for newly generated blocks. Its seed remains repeatable, and the full mix can still guide energy. Saved blocks are unchanged until explicitly regenerated. **Suggest from audio** retains the earlier feature-based suggestions described below.

With a drum stem alone, suggestions use local beat rate, attack density and strength. With a full mix, compact features describe sustained energy, energy changes, bass power, brightness and attack activity. Combined with drum strength, density and offbeat hits, these rank ten shapes every eight beats: Smooth Bounce, Sine Wave, Half Stroke, Staircase Up/Down, Double Tap, Quick Rise, Hold High, Accent & Echo and Swing. For example, a build favors Staircase Up; sustained passages favor Sine Wave; dense attacks favor Double Tap. Older saved random settings without a catalogue family retain their previous small pool when used directly; new UI previews save the selected family.

Timing always comes from the drum input, including when using Drum peaks or Tempo grid. Full-mix events never move beat markers. Blue shows the drum waveform, purple the full-mix envelope and gold the drum timing. Phrases with less than 80% full-mix coverage use drum-only analysis and say so in **Why these shapes?**. Use the independent offsets to correct alignment for separately trimmed stems or files with different leading silence.

These are signal-based heuristics, not learned recognition of melody, genre, choreography or verse/chorus boundaries. An eight-beat phrase is a grouping on the chosen timing grid. Review the preview and try half/double timing or a manual tempo when the estimated pulse is wrong. The 28 audio shapes include Triangle, Sine Wave and 26 additional rhythm templates; the ordinary pattern generator offers all 40 shapes.

**Follow audio energy** varies amplitude using cycle-level energy, with smooth transitions. Dual mode combines 70% full-mix energy with 30% drum energy where the mix covers the cycle. Quiet drum cycles or a silent full mix fade toward the chosen center, including with Follow audio energy disabled. **Stroke length** sets the full travel in the 0–100 script range (80% is the previous amplitude of 40). Energy and shapes may reduce that travel; center and 0–100 clipping still apply. Old saved amplitude values reopen at the equivalent stroke length. Exact collinear point reduction keeps generated files smaller without altering their piecewise-linear curves.

Audio blocks, both analyses, both offsets, choice explanations and generation settings are saved with the project and shared between linked editors. The raw audio is not uploaded or embedded. Normal playback uses the video audio; **Listen rhythm** pauses it and opens a separate percussion player. Reopening the project does not need either audio file to regenerate blocks from saved analysis. Replacing or removing an input or changing its offset leaves saved blocks intact until you preview and replace a block. Uncheck **Use full mix for shape and energy** to compare drum-only suggestions without removing the mix.

For server-loaded videos, the video button extracts and caches a small mono WAV under the ComfyUI output directory's `sam3d_funscript/audio-previews` folder. Only audio is decoded and transferred for analysis; the browser does not download the entire video for this operation. File size and modification time invalidate the cache. The first audio stream is used, and timestamp gaps are preserved. In an offline viewer, first choose the source video in the preview; its soundtrack is decoded locally with no upload. For stabilized offline projects, choose the original video in the Original view. Unsupported or silent videos show an error and keep the previous analysis. Cancelling or changing projects also leaves saved audio inputs and blocks intact.

**Remove selected range** removes only the marked piece from the audio row and keeps the neighboring audio. Copies already in Main are independent: use **Applied patterns → Remove pattern** to restore the previous Main section, or use **Undo**. Undo also covers audio import, offset changes and block edits. A same-video processing rerun preserves the audio lane; changing to a different video starts a separate project.
